    Learn to check a trailer, step by step

    Drivers send us photos of their paperwork and a video of the trailer. You check both and decide: is everything OK, or is something wrong? No trucking experience needed. Every lesson uses real papers and real videos from the job.

    The golden ruleFind it, be sure, tell the right people. Nothing rolls with an open issue.
    Not sure? Ask the group.

    Every truck group has other teams in it (dispatch, PTI, maintenance, tires, safety and more). If you have a question, ask: they will help you.

    Sure there's an issue? Tell dispatch or the updaters.

    Tell the dispatcher or an updater exactly what is wrong, so they can inform the broker (for Amazon loads: ROC). The broker asks for the fix or sends someone.

    We can't roll with any issue.

    The trailer does not move until the issue is fixed. You never fix anything yourself: your job is to spot it and tell people.

    Every trailer check, in 5 steps

    1. RegistrationPlate, VIN, expiry date
    2. InspectionDate, passed, matches the trailer
    3. Bill of ladingWeight, hazmat, destination, date
    4. VideoLights, tires, brakes, doors
    5. InformReport every issue, create the ticket

    Lesson 2 of 11Paperwork

    Trailer registration

    Every state's registration looks different. Don't worry about the layout. You are always hunting for the same three things.

    Key points
    • Check 3 things: plate, VIN and expiry date.
    • "NO EXPIRY" or "PERMANENT" means it is valid.
    • A temporary permit: valid until its date, and inform about the expiration date.
    • No plate: it can't roll, in any situation.
    • Expired, or the expiry can't be read: inform or ask for a clearer photo.
    1 · Plate numberMatches the plate in the video.
    2 · VIN17 characters. Must match the inspection.
    3 · Expiry dateMust be in the future. "NO EXPIRY" and "PERMANENT" are valid.

    Practice on real registrations

    Every registration looks different, but you only look for the plate, the VIN and the expiry. Pick an example, then click each field to see where it is.

    Try it: VIN compare

    Paste the VIN from the registration and the VIN from the inspection paper (or the manufacturer plate). Any character that doesn't match turns red.

    How to compare a VIN without mistakes

    Read the VIN in small groups, the way you read a phone number:

    1DW1 · A532 · 6NBA · 85270

    • Check all 17 characters. The last 6 are the ones that change most.
    • A VIN never uses the letters I, O or Q. If you see one, it's a 1 or a 0.
    • One character different = not a match → inform.

    Expiry: what counts as valid

    • A date in the future, like 02/29/2036
    • "NO EXPIRY", "PERMANENT" or "PERMANENT TRAILER"
    • A date that already passed → inform
    • Expiry box empty: valid
    • An application receipt: accepted, check the plate and VIN
    • A temporary permit: valid until its Exp date. Inform the dispatcher or an updater about the expiration date.
    • Expiry can't be read → ask the driver for a clearer photo
    No plate
    If the trailer has no plate, it can't roll, in any situation. Inform the dispatcher or an updater right away.
    Watch out
    Don't mix up dates. "Date registered", "purchase date" and "start year" are not the expiry. Look for the words Expires, Expiration or Validity period.

    Lesson 3 of 11Paperwork

    Annual inspection

    Every trailer needs a safety inspection once a year. The driver shows it to you as a paper, a sticker on the trailer, or both.

    Key points
    • Dated within the last 12 months.
    • It must show the trailer passed.
    • Match the paper to the trailer: VIN, unit # or plate.
    • On a sticker, only the date matters.
    • The manufacturer plate is only needed when you have to check the VIN, not every time.
    • A valid sticker means it can roll, but we still need the paper: ask dispatch or the updaters to request it from the broker.
    Key rule
    Sticker = OK to roll. Paper is still needed. If the driver only has a valid sticker, the trailer can roll, but ask the dispatcher or an updater to request the inspection paper from the broker.
    1 · DateWithin the last 12 months. Older than that = expired → inform.
    2 · PASSEDIt must say passed (or have the pass box ticked). Failed or blank → inform.
    3 · It's this trailerMatch the VIN, unit # or plate # to the registration. One match is enough.

    Try it: is the inspection still valid?

    Type the inspection date from the paper or the sticker. It is valid for 12 months from that date.

    Quick example

    TodayInspection dateResultMatches registration?Decision
    Nov 18, 2026Mar 3, 2026PASSEDVIN matches OK
    Nov 18, 2026Oct 2, 2025PASSEDVIN matches Over 12 months → inform
    Nov 18, 2026Jun 9, 2026PASSEDUnit # is different Wrong trailer? → inform

    Inspection stickers and the VIN plate

    Reading a sticker

    On a sticker, only the date is needed. Most stickers are punched: a hole on the year, the month and the day. Some are written (like 8-26), and some show the next due date instead. Within 12 months of the inspection, or before the due date: OK.

    The manufacturer plate (VIN plate)

    Not needed every time. Only ask for it when you need to check the VIN (for example, the VIN is missing or doesn't match on the papers). It's the metal plate from the factory, near the front of the trailer, with the trailer's VIN. The "date of manufacture" on it is not the inspection date.

    Practice on real inspection papers

    Every shop uses a different form. Pick a paper, then click each field on the right to see where it is.

    Watch out
    A date printed at the very bottom of a form is often the form number or revision date, not the inspection date. Look for the field called Date or Inspection date, or the date in the inspector's certification.
    Can't read it?
    Handwritten dates are often hard to read. Don't guess: ask the driver for a clear, flat photo in good light. If you still can't confirm the date or the result, inform.

    Lesson 4 of 11Paperwork

    Bill of lading (BOL)

    The BOL tells you what's inside the trailer. Every company prints it differently, so again you hunt for the same four things.

    Key points
    • Over 30,000 lb: the driver goes to a CAT scale.
    • Any hazmat: inform right away.
    • Note the destination state for KPRA, and check the date is today.
    • Empty trailer: no BOL needed.
    1 · Total weightThe weight of the whole load. Over 30,000 lb → driver goes to a CAT scale.
    2 · HazmatAny hazmat at all → inform immediately.
    3 · DestinationCity and state. You need the state for the KPRA check.
    4 · DateShould be today. Not today → inform.
    Empty
    Empty trailers do not need a BOL. No load = no BOL. That's OK.
    Hazmat
    Look for an X in the HM column, UN numbers (like UN1090), or the words Hazardous Materials. Even a tiny amount counts. Inform right away.

    Practice on real BOLs

    Weight trap
    On the Home Depot BOL, "TOTAL WEIGHT: 95.46 LBS" is only the hazmat items. The real load weight is further down: 27,477.7 lb. Always look for the biggest "total" for the whole trailer.

    Lesson 5 of 11Paperwork

    Scale tickets: balance the axles

    Every load over 30,000 lb goes to a CAT scale. If one axle is over its limit and the gross is under 80,000, the driver can fix it by sliding. Your job is to read the ticket and tell the driver exactly what to slide.

    Key points
    • Limits: steer 12,000 · drive 34,000 · trailer 34,000 · gross 80,000.
    • Always slide the tandems first. The fifth wheel only if needed.
    • Steer over and the fifth wheel can't slide: ask for the driver door sticker photo, and check the state's max steer weight. The lower one counts.
    • Check the fuel level first. Diesel ≈ 7.1 lb/gal; 200 gal ≈ 1,420 lb on the drives. Low fuel: leave room. Full tank and legal: leave it as it is.
    • Can't slide any more to make room? Tag the fuel team with a max fuel level.
    • Tandems can't slide, or gross over 80,000: inform. The load goes back to the shipper to be restacked.
    12,000Steer axle

    Front of the truck.

    34,000Drive axles

    Back of the truck.

    34,000Trailer axles

    The tandems.

    80,000Gross

    Everything together. Over this, sliding can't help.

    Watch: fixing a scale ticket

    How each slide moves weight

    Fix #1 · always first

    Trailer tandems

    Moves weight between the drive and trailer axles. About 250 lb per hole.

    • Forward (toward the truck): drive → trailer.
    • Back: trailer → drive. Makes the KPRA longer, so check the state limit.
    Fix #2 · only if needed

    Fifth wheel

    Moves weight between the steer and drive axles. About 500 lb per notch.

    • Back: steer → drive.
    • Forward: drive → steer.

    Practice on real scale tickets

    Try it: scale balancer

    Type the weights from a ticket. The balancer suggests the fix, tandems first, and writes the message for the driver. Or move the sliders yourself.

    Load a ticket:

    Steer over and the fifth wheel can't slide

    Ask the driver for a photo of the sticker on the driver door. It shows the steer (front) axle rating for that truck. On this Volvo the front axle is rated 13,220 lb.

    If the steer weight is under that rating and under the state limit (below), send the photo with the scale ticket and inform the dispatcher or an updater.

    Ask the driver like this

    "Please send a clear photo of the sticker on your driver door that shows the axle weight ratings."

    Steer axle: every state has its own maximum

    The door sticker is the truck's own rating, but it is not always the final word. Each state has a maximum steer axle weight, and it can be lower than the sticker.

    • Check the states on the route.
    • The steer must be under the sticker rating and under the state limit. The lower number counts.
    • Example: sticker 13,220, but California allows 12,500. A steer at 12,660 is over in California.
    • Over either one: inform.

    Not sure about a state? Ask the group.

    Can't fix
    Tandems can't slide, or the gross is over 80,000: the weight can't be fixed. Inform the dispatcher or an updater right away: the truck has to go back to the shipper to restack the load.
    Fuel first
    Check the fuel level before you decide. Diesel weighs about 7.1 lb per gallon. Most trucks hold about 200 gallons in two tanks, so a full fill-up adds about 1,420 lb. The tanks sit just in front of the drive axles, so fuel makes the drives heavier.
    • Weight still to come = gallons to fill × 7.1. Half a tank (100 gal) ≈ 710 lb.
    • Low fuel: slide so the drives keep that much room under 34,000.
    • Full tank: no room needed. If every axle is legal, leave it as it is.
    Fuel team
    If the tandems can't slide any more to make room for fuel, tag the fuel team in the group and give them a max fuel level. Room on the drives ÷ 7.1 = gallons the truck can still take. Example: fuel 30% (60 gal), drives 33,650 → 350 lb room ≈ 50 gal → about 110 gal = "keep the fuel at 50% max".

    Lesson 6 of 11Route rules

    KPRA and California

    Some states limit how far the back wheels can be from the kingpin (where the trailer hooks onto the truck). The destination on the BOL tells you if this matters.

    Key points
    • Find the destination state on the BOL.
    • State on the list: ask the driver to measure kingpin to center of the last axle.
    • Over the limit: inform. The tandems can usually slide.
    • Going to or in California without side skirts: just inform.

    How to check it, in 4 steps

    1. Find the stateRead the destination on the BOL.
    2. Is it on the list?Not on the list: nothing to do for KPRA.
    3. Ask the driver to measureKingpin to the center of the last axle, with a photo of the tape.
    4. CompareOver the limit: tell dispatch or the updaters. The trailer can't roll until it's fixed.
    Ask the driver like this

    "Brother, your load goes to New Jersey. Please make sure the distance between the kingpin and the center of the last axle does not exceed 41 ft, and send us a photo of the tape."

    Tip
    Most trailers have sliding tandems. If it's over the limit, the driver can usually slide the tandems forward and measure again.

    Illinois: what Class I, II and III mean

    Illinois sorts its roads into classes. The KPRA limit depends on which class of road the truck drives on.

    ClassWhat roadsKPRA limit

    Lesson 7 of 11The trailer

    The walkaround video

    The driver walks around the trailer and films it. You watch and tick off each part. If you can't see something clearly, don't guess. Ask the driver to show it again.

    Key points
    • Watch the whole walkaround, with the light test on.
    • Check tires, rims, brakes, air bags, lines, mudflaps and doors.
    • A part is missing or unclear: ask or call the driver.
    • Any issue: inform.

    Animated guide

    Watch this first (with voice-over, subtitles and sound effects). It shows every part of the trailer check in order, with a good vs inform close-up for each part. Click a chapter to jump straight to it.

    The perfect PTI video

    This is what a complete trailer video looks like, step by step. Learn this order. When a driver's video is missing a step, ask for that step.

    Most steps are one trailer filmed in daytime with the light test button on. The tires, underneath, mudflap and ABS steps come from other driver videos. A real video is one trailer, filmed in one go.

    Real videos: complete or not?

    Drivers often send part of the walkaround. Some videos are tires only, or only underneath. Look at what each one shows and what you would still ask for.

    Daytime light test

    • Whole trailer and unit number
    • Air lines and glad hands, close up
    • Both sides and DOT tape
    • Rear with lights ON, rear tape
    • Plate
    • Nothing underneath
    • No tread check
    Almost perfect. Lights are hard to see in daylight, so watch the rear closely. Ask for brakes/suspension if needed.

    Daytime walkaround

    • Air lines and glad hands
    • Both sides, DOT tape, landing gear
    • Mudflaps and tires
    • Rear, plate (empty trailer, doors open)
    • No light test: no lights at all
    • Nothing underneath
    Not complete. Ask for the light test video and brakes/suspension.

    Sides and rear

    • Sides and DOT tape
    • Tires (close look at the tread)
    • Rear doors, bumper and rear tape
    • Plate
    • No front, no air lines
    • No lights
    Not complete. Ask for the front with air lines, and the light test.

    Underneath only

    • Brakes and air chambers
    • Suspension and axles
    • Inner tires
    • Everything above the frame
    Good part video. Covers brakes and suspension. You still need the rest of the walkaround.

    Tires only, with a gauge

    • Tread depth measured on each tire
    • Rims, hubs and lug nuts
    • Everything else
    Perfect tire video. This is exactly how to show tread depth. Read the gauge: 2/32 in or less = change now.

    Night walkaround

    • Air lines and glad hands
    • Rear lights and plate light on
    • Sides too dark and too fast
    • Tires and underneath not clear
    Not clear enough. Ask the driver to show the lights from all sides again, slowly, with a flashlight.
    Ask like this
    "Brother, please send the PTI video for the trailer with the light test button on. We must see all lights working. Also make sure to show brakes, suspension, tires and rims in the video."

    Every light, in one picture

    What to check, step by step

    The same 7 steps as the video, in the same order.

    1Front

    Air lines & glad hands

    Red (emergency) and blue (service) lines at the front.
    lines are cut or cracked, or the rubber seals are missing or damaged.

    Catwalk lines

    The air and electrical lines cross the deck behind the cab. They must stay 6 in or more above it.
    a line touching, dragging or resting on the deck.

    Front clearance L & R

    At the top front corners of the trailer, where the front wall meets the roof. One on each side.
    either one is out.

    2Sides

    Trailer body

    Walls, roof and front: no cracks anywhere.
    any crack in the body.

    Side DOT tape

    Red and white reflective strip along the bottom of both sides. It must cover at least 50% of the side, spread along the whole length.
    less than half the side, missing, peeling, or so dirty it doesn't reflect.

    Side marker L & R

    In the middle of each side.
    either one is out.

    Side clearance L & R

    At the bottom rear corner of each side.
    either one is out.

    Landing gear

    Legs fully up, nothing bent, and the crank handle secured in its holder.
    a bent leg, or the handle hanging loose.

    Side skirts

    If the trailer has them: on both sides or none, secure and straight. A 53 ft box trailer going to (or in) California with no side skirts: inform.
    a skirt on one side only, bent, cracked or hanging, or no skirts going to California.

    3Tires

    Tires & rims

    Tread depth, air pressure (PSI) and damage. Pressure should be 100–105 PSI; 105 is the max on the trailer. The driver shows the tread gauge reading clearly on camera. 2/32 in is the legal minimum.
    tread is 3/32 in (change soon) or 2/32 in or less (change before it rolls), pressure under 100 or over 105 PSI, a tire looks flat or low, or there are cuts, bulges, cracked rims or missing lug nuts.
    Tread tip
    No tread gauge? Tell the driver to buy one. Until then, he checks with a penny before he rolls: put Lincoln's head down into the groove. If you can see the top of his head, the tread is 2/32 in or less → inform.

    4Mudflaps

    Mudflaps L & R

    Behind the last tires on both sides, on a bracket mounted on the tandem, so they move with it when it slides. The bracket swings freely.
    missing, torn, or fixed to the trailer body.

    5Underneath

    Brakes & suspension

    Brake chambers, push rods and slack adjusters. The driver films the brake drums close up: brake pad at least 1/4 in, no cracks, no oil. Air bags full, checked down to their base. Springs whole, shocks dry. Lines and wires held by clips, each on its own path.
    hissing air, a thin or oily brake pad, a flat or torn air bag, a cracked spring, a leaking shock, or a crossed or hanging line.

    Slider pins all 4

    They lock the sliding tandem to the trailer. All 4 must be fully in.
    a pin out is URGENT: the tandem can slide. Inform right away; the truck does not roll until it is fixed.

    6Rear

    Light test

    The driver pushes the light test button so every light at the rear can be checked: tail lights on, turn signals and brake lights taking turns.
    no light test in the video: ask the driver for it.

    Tail lights L & R

    The brake lights and turn signals at the rear, when they're not blinking. They must stay on.
    any one is out.

    LED dots

    Many rear lights are made of small LED dots. At least 50% of the dots must work.
    fewer than half the dots work, and any dots out: inform, even if the light is on.

    Brake lights & turn signals L & R

    Same lights at the rear, same color. The driver pushes the light test button: the turn signals and brake lights take turns: turn signals on, then off, then brake lights on, then off, and it repeats. When they're not blinking, they stay on as tail lights.
    any one doesn't blink.

    3 middle rear ID lights

    Three lights in a row, top middle of the rear.
    any of the 3 is out.

    2 corner rear ID lights

    Top rear corners. Not every trailer has them.
    the trailer has them and either one is out. If they are there, both must be on.

    Plate light

    White, above or beside the plate. The plate must be readable.
    it's out.

    Rear DOT tape

    On the rear doors, the underride guard (bumper bar) and top corners. The tape on the underride guard must not be damaged at all.
    missing or damaged, and any damage on the underride guard tape.

    No placards attached

    We don't haul hazmat, so a placard on the trailer means something is wrong. Placards are diamond-shaped hazmat signs with a color, a symbol and a class number at the bottom. Look for them on the front, both sides and the rear. An empty placard holder (just an outline frame) is fine.
    you see a placard. Company logos are not placards; if you're unsure, inform.

    Rear doors

    Closed, every handle locked in its latch, every lock rod cam locked in its keeper at the top, and the hinges fully secured.
    a loose handle, an open door, a cam out of its keeper, or a loose or broken hinge.

    7ABS

    ABS light

    On the driver side of the trailer, near the rear. It must turn on, then turn off (self-test).
    it stays on, or never turns on.

    ABS light self-test (real clip)

    The ABS light comes on for a few seconds, then goes off. That is a pass. If it stays on or never comes on → inform.

    Lesson 8 of 11The trailer

    Container on a chassis

    Some loads are a shipping container on a chassis. Check it the same way as a trailer (tires, brakes, mudflaps, DOT tape, light test). These are the differences.

    Key points
    • Rear locks: twist lock head horizontal (vertical = not locked), lever to the front, pin zip-tied.
    • Front locks, both sides: lever down, pin zip-tied.
    • 3 ID lights above the plate; front clearance low on the front corner.
    • Papers: in the holder at the front of the chassis, above the landing gear.

    Watch the chassis part

    What is different on a chassis

    Chassis papers

    The chassis registration and inspection papers are kept in the paper holder at the front of the chassis, where the gooseneck meets the frame, just above the landing gear. Ask the driver to open it and send clear photos.
    no papers in the holder, or the photos are unreadable: ask the driver again.

    Rear corner locks

    Look at the twist lock head inside the corner hole: horizontal = locked, vertical = not locked. The lever under the chassis must face the front (toward the truck), and its pin must be zip-tied.
    a vertical head, a lever pointing back, or no zip tie: not secured. The driver must twist the lock and zip-tie it.

    Front corner locks both sides

    At the front corners of the chassis. The lever must be down, and its pin zip-tied to hold it down.
    a lever not down, or a missing zip tie.

    3 ID lights

    Not on top like a trailer: three small lights above the plate, on the chassis light bar.
    any of the 3 is out.

    Front clearance L & R

    Low, on the front corner of the chassis, not up on top of the container.
    either one is out.

    Side marker L & R

    In the middle of the chassis rail.
    either one is out.

    Side clearance L & R

    At the rear corner, on the corner casting.
    either one is out.

    ABS light

    Under the side near the rear, on the chassis frame. Same self-test: on, then off.
    it stays on, or never turns on.

    Lesson 9 of 11Act and practice

    Inform the right people, and call the driver

    Finding a problem only helps if the right people hear about it. When you are sure there is an issue, tell the dispatcher or an updater so they can inform the broker. When you need something from the driver, ask or call.

    Key points
    • Sure there is an issue? Tell the dispatcher or an updater, so they inform the broker.
    • Not sure? Ask in the group: the other teams will help.
    • Be short and specific: say exactly what is wrong.
    • Issues or not, create the ticket on the website.
    • Never write gtg in the group.

    Dispatcher

    Plans the load. Needs to know before the truck leaves.

    Driver

    Can fix many things on the spot: retake a photo, slide the axles, go to a scale.

    Updaters

    Keep the broker and the records up to date.

    Calling the driver
    
    
    
      
      
        
    1. Say who you are."Hi, this is [your name] from UGL Dispatch. Are you the driver for trailer [number]?"
      2. 
        
    2. Say what you found. One thing at a time, in simple words."I checked your paperwork. The registration photo is blurry and I can't read the expiry date."
      3. 
        
    3. Ask for one specific thing."Can you send a clear photo of the registration, flat, in good light?"
      4. 
        
    4. Check they understood. Repeat numbers back in small groups."So that's plate 5-7-1, 6-5-7-1. Correct?"
      5. 
        
    5. Say what happens next, and thank them."Thanks. I'll update dispatch as soon as I get it."
      6. 
      
    
      
    
        
    Do
    Be polite, short and specific. Be patient: drivers are often outside, in the dark, or busy. Write down what they told you.
    
        
    Don't
    Don't guess. Don't argue. Don't promise the load is approved. Never say "it's fine" when you're not sure.

    ReferenceThe trailer
    
    
    Trailer types
    
    
    Know what you are looking at before you check it. Most of our loads use the 4 main trailers. The others come up sometimes, and some have their own rules.
    
    
    Key points
    • Main trailers: Dry Van, Reefer, Flatbed, Chassis Container.
    • We don't take tri-axle trailers at all.
    • We don't take tankers at all, loaded or empty.
    • Swing doors: hinges fully secured. Roll-up door: only if it is pre-loaded.
    • Not sure what type it is? Ask the group.
    

    
    The 4 main trailers we deal with
    
    
    
      
    
        
        
    Dry Van Trailer
    The most common trailer: a closed box, usually 53 ft, with swing doors at the back. It carries general freight, like most Amazon loads.
    
          
    • Closed box, no machine on the front
    • Swing doors (sometimes a roll-up door) at the rear
    
          
    What we check: The full walkaround from the Walkaround video lesson: front, sides, tires, mudflaps, underneath, rear, ABS.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Reefer Trailer
    A refrigerated trailer. It keeps the load cold or frozen (food, medicine). The walls are insulated.
    
          
    • A refrigeration unit on the front wall
    • A small fuel tank for that unit under the trailer
    
          
    What we check: Same walkaround as a dry van.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Flatbed Trailer
    An open deck with no walls and no roof. The load is tied down with straps or chains.
    
          
    • Flat open deck
    • No doors, so no door or cam check
    
          
    What we check: Same tires, lights, brakes, mudflaps, DOT tape and ABS checks. There are no rear doors to check.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Chassis Container Trailer
    A shipping container sitting on a chassis (a frame on wheels). The container is locked to the chassis with twist locks at the corners.
    
          
    • Ribbed steel container on a separate frame
    • Twist locks at the corners
    
          
    What we check: Same walkaround, plus the chassis checks: twist locks, zip ties, ID lights above the plate, chassis papers. Open the Chassis lesson →
    
      
    
    
    

    
    Door types
    
    
    Box trailers come with swing doors or a roll-up door. We take both, with these rules.
    
    
    
      
    
        
        
    Swing doors
    Two doors that open outward on hinges. The most common.
    
          
    • Handles locked in their latches, cams in their keepers
    • Hinges fully secured: every hinge tight, no missing bolts, nothing broken
    
          
    Inform: a loose or broken hinge, or missing bolts.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Roll-up door
    One door that rolls up into the roof, like a garage door.
    
          
    • We take it only if it is pre-loaded (loaded before the pickup)
    
          
    Inform: a roll-up door trailer that is not pre-loaded.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    Other types
    
    
    Less common. Learn to recognize them. We don't take tankers or tri-axle trailers at all.
    
    
    
      
    
        
        
    Tri-Axle
    A trailer with 3 axles instead of 2, for heavier loads.
    How to spot it: Count the axles at the back: 3 in a row.
    We don't take it at all. A tri-axle trailer: inform.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Tanker
    A round tank that carries liquids: water, milk, oil, fuel, chemicals.
    How to spot it: Round, shiny tank with valves and hoses.
    We don't take it at all. Loaded or empty: inform.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Frac Tank
    A large steel tank on wheels that holds water or fluids at oil and gas sites. It is not a tanker.
    How to spot it: Big box-shaped steel tank, often with stairs on the side.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Step Deck
    A flatbed with two levels: a short upper deck at the front and a lower main deck, for taller loads.
    How to spot it: A step down in the deck behind the front.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Dry Bulk (Pneumatic)
    A tank with cone-shaped hoppers for dry loads like cement, sand, flour or plastic pellets. It unloads with air pressure.
    How to spot it: Tank with hoppers (cones) underneath.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    RGN (Removable Gooseneck)
    A low trailer for heavy machinery. The front (the gooseneck) comes off so the deck drops to the ground and equipment drives on.
    How to spot it: Very low deck and a big gooseneck at the front.
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
    Conestoga
    A flatbed with a tarp cover that slides open and closed on a rolling frame, like a curtain. It protects the load while still loading from the side or top.
    How to spot it: A tarp roof and sides on arched bows, bunched at one end when open.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    Trailers we don't take
    
    
    
      
    1
    Tankers: never
    We don't take tankers at all: loaded or empty, with or without the tanker endorsement. Inform right away.
    
      
    2
    Tri-axle trailers: never
    We don't take tri-axle trailers at all, to any state. Inform right away.

    Cheat sheet
    
    
    Golden rule
    Not sure → ask the group. Sure there's an issue → tell the dispatcher or an updater to inform the broker. Nothing rolls with an open issue.
    
    
    
      Check a truckMark each part as you go. The messages write themselves.
      
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
          
          
    
          
     
    
          
          
    
          
     
    
          
        
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Registration
    • Plate · VIN · Expiry
    • "NO EXPIRY" / "PERMANENT" = valid
    • Expired → inform · empty expiry box = valid · application receipt = accepted
    • Temporary permit: valid until its date, inform about the expiration
    • No plate = can't roll, in any situation
    • VIN: 17 characters, no I, O or Q
    
      
    Annual inspection
    • Within the last 12 months
    • Says PASSED
    • VIN, unit # or plate matches the registration
    • Sticker = OK to roll, but request the paper from the broker
    
      
    Bill of lading
    • Total weight · Hazmat · Destination · Date
    • Hazmat → inform immediately
    • Over 30,000 lb → CAT scale
    • Date should be today
    • Empty trailer → no BOL needed
    
      
    KPRA limits
    • 40' CA
    • 40'6" MI (min 37'6")
    • 41' AL DC FL MD NC NH NJ PA RI SC TN VA VT
    • 42'6" IL Class III: smaller roads (45'6" on Class I/II: Interstates, tollways, approved state highways)
    • 43' CT IN ME MN NY WI
    
      
    California
    • KPRA max 40 ft
    • Going to or in CA with no side skirts → inform
    
      
    Trailer
    • Side DOT tape: at least 50% of the side, spread along it · rear tape · underride guard tape: no damage at all
    • No placards (front, sides, rear)
    • Mudflaps L & R
    • Body: no cracks anywhere
    • Side skirts: on both sides or none, secure and straight
    • Mudflaps on the tandem, not fixed to the trailer
    • Landing gear up, crank handle in its holder
    • Air lines and glad hand seals: no cuts, no cracks
    
      
    Tires
    • Tread: 3/32 in → inform, change soon · 2/32 in or less → change before it rolls (legal minimum)
    • Driver shows the gauge reading clearly on camera
    • Gauge: pin fully in, base flat, pin straight down in a main groove (never at an angle). Green good, yellow low, red replace
    • No gauge: driver buys one; penny check before he rolls
    • Pressure 100–105 PSI (105 max): under 100 or over 105 → inform
    • Rims: no cracks or bends, every lug nut tight, no oil from the hub seal
    
      
    Catwalk + underneath
    • Air and electrical lines 6 in or more above the catwalk, never touching
    • Brake pad at least 1/4 in, no cracks, no oil
    • Air bags full, checked down to their base; shocks dry; spring leaves not cracked or shifted; brake chambers tight
    • Hubs dry, hub caps whole (no oil on the rims)
    • Lines and wires underneath held by clips or support springs, on their own path: not crossed, twisted, hanging or cut
    • All 4 slider pins fully in · a pin out = URGENT, inform right away
    
      
    Rear doors
    • Closed and secured
    • Every handle locked in its latch
    • Lock rod cams inside their keepers (top of the doors)
    • Hinges fully secured
    • Roll-up door: only if pre-loaded
    • Upper corner tape is white
    
      
    Lights
    • Front clearance L/R
    • Side marker L/R · side clearance L/R
    • ABS: on, then off
    • Tail lights L/R: on · brake + turn L/R: blinking (light test button) · plate light (white)
    • 3 middle rear ID
    • 2 corner rear ID: if there, both on
    • LED dots: at least 50% working · any dots out → inform, even if the light is on
    
      
    Scale and fuel
    • Limits: steer 12,000 · drive 34,000 · trailer 34,000 · gross 80,000
    • Fuel first: diesel ≈ 7.1 lb/gal · 200 gal ≈ 1,420 lb, all on the drives
    • Tandems first (≈ 250 lb per hole) · fifth wheel only if needed (≈ 500 lb per notch)
    • Steer over, fifth wheel can't slide → driver door sticker photo, and check the state's max steer weight: the lower one counts
    • Can't slide enough for fuel → tag the fuel team with a max fuel level
    • Tandems can't slide, or gross over 80,000 → inform: back to the shipper to restack
    
      
    Team rules
    • Not sure → ask the group
    • Sure → tell the dispatcher or an updater, who inform the broker (ROC for Amazon)
    • Issues or not → create the ticket on the Trailer Check website
    • Never write gtg: only the dispatcher says G2G
    • Missing a part → ask or call the driver
    
      
    Trailer types
    • Main: Dry Van · Reefer · Flatbed · Chassis Container
    • Tankers: we don't take them at all (loaded or empty)
    • Tri-axle: we don't take them at all
    
      
    Chassis (intermodal)
    • Papers: holder at the front of the chassis, above the landing gear
    • Rear locks: head horizontal (vertical = not locked), lever to the front, pin zip-tied
    • Front locks (both sides): lever down, pin zip-tied
    • 3 ID lights above the plate
    • Front clearance low on the front corner
    • ABS under the side, near the rear
